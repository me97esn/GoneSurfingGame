# Rail Lift Specification

## Overview
Rail lift is generated by the curved surfaces of the surfboard rails as water flows past them. The rails have two independent curvatures that each generate lift based on Bernoulli's principle.

## Physics Principle
Rail curvature creates lift perpendicular to the rail surface (pointing away from the board) when water flows along the curved surface. This is Bernoulli's principle applied in two dimensions.

## Two Independent Lift Components

### 1. Horizontal Curvature Lift (Front-to-Back Flow)
**View:** Top-down view of the surfboard
**Curvature:** Rail curve in the XY plane (horizontal plane)
**Water Flow:** Front → Back along the rail
**Lift Direction:** Perpendicular to rail, pointing outward from board

When the surfboard moves forward through water, the water flows from front to back along the curved rail surface (as seen from above). This horizontal curvature generates lift outward from the board.

**Angle of Attack Measurement:**
- Project relative water velocity onto the XY plane (horizontal plane)
- Calculate angle between this projection and the rail's forward direction
- Use `abs()` of the cosine since we care about flow magnitude, not direction
- Valid range: cos(angle) > 0.87 (angle < ~30 degrees)

### 2. Vertical Curvature Lift (Bottom-to-Top Flow)
**View:** Front-on view of the rail
**Curvature:** Rail curve in the XZ plane (vertical cross-section)
**Water Flow:** Bottom → Top along the rail
**Lift Direction:** Perpendicular to rail, pointing outward from board

When water flows upward past the rail (from bottom to top), the vertical curvature of the rail generates lift outward from the board.

**Angle of Attack Measurement:**
- Project relative water velocity onto the XZ plane (vertical plane perpendicular to forward)
- Calculate angle between this projection and the rail's upward direction
- Use `abs()` of the cosine since we care about flow magnitude, not direction
- Valid range: cos(angle) > 0.87 (angle < ~30 degrees)

## Lift Coefficient Function
Both components use the same lift coefficient approximation based on angle of attack:

- **Function:** `apxLiftCoefficient(cosAngleOfAttack)`
- **Input range:** 0.87 to 1.0 (corresponding to 30° to 0° angle of attack)
- **Behavior:**
  - 0 lift at angles > 30° (cos < 0.87)
  - Increases from 0 at ~30°
  - Maximum around 10° (cos ≈ 0.984)
  - Returns to 0.5 at 0° (cos = 1.0)

## Total Rail Lift Calculation

```cpp
railLiftAmount = horizontalCurvatureLift + verticalCurvatureLift
```

Both components:
1. Calculate their own angle of attack
2. Check if cos(angle) > 0.87
3. If yes, apply lift coefficient function
4. Scale by velocity squared and other factors
5. Add together for total lift magnitude

The combined lift force is applied in a single direction: perpendicular to the rail surface, pointing away from the board.

Original heading-based reasoning, kept for historical context:

Use board heading direction (`forwards.Y`) to determine which rail engages the wave:

- **Waves travel:** +X direction (world space)
- **Left rail engaged when:** Board heads toward +Y (right) → `forwards.Y > threshold`
- **Right rail engaged when:** Board heads toward -Y (left) → `forwards.Y < -threshold`

This simple heading check determines which rail is "into" the wave face.

## Implementation Requirements

### Coordinate System
- **forwards:** Rail's forward direction (toward nose)
- **left:** Direction perpendicular to rail, pointing left from board center
- **up:** Direction perpendicular to board surface, pointing up

### For Left Rail:
- Lift direction: `+left` (away from board)
- Applies when: `forwards.Y > headingThreshold`

### For Right Rail:
- Lift direction: `-left` (away from board)
- Applies when: `forwards.Y < -headingThreshold`

## Calculation Steps

### Horizontal Curvature Component:
1. Project `relativeWaterVelocity` onto XY plane (remove Z component)
2. Calculate cos(angle) between projected flow and `forwards` direction
3. Take `abs()` of result
4. If `abs(cos) > 0.87`: calculate lift using coefficient function
5. Scale by: velocity squared, lift magnitude, amount underwater

### Vertical Curvature Component:
1. Project `relativeWaterVelocity` onto XZ plane (remove Y component)
2. Calculate cos(angle) between projected flow and `up` direction
3. Take `abs()` of result
4. If `abs(cos) > 0.87`: calculate lift using coefficient function
5. Scale by: velocity squared, lift magnitude, amount underwater

### Final Force:
```cpp
FVector railLiftDirection = (side == ESide::VE_Left) ? left : -left;
FVector railLiftForce = railLiftDirection * railLiftAmount;
```

## Key Differences from Bottom Lift
- **Bottom lift:** Single component, downward direction, only on bottom surface
- **Rail lift:** Two components (horizontal + vertical), outward direction, only on rails
- **Both:** Use same lift coefficient function based on angle of attack

- **Color:** Cyan (to distinguish from bottom lift)

- **Magnitude:** Sum of both horizontal and vertical components
